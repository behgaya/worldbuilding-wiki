Zayn Alaric Wells is the moral and emotional backbone of Team Vulcan, a hunter who believes strength exists to protect rather than intimidate. To him, monster hunting is not a path to glory or recognition, but a public service. Every mission is ultimately about people: the civilians who need help, the communities that depend on hunters, and the teammates who trust him to bring them home safely.

While others may see him as a natural leader, Zayn sees leadership as a responsibility rather than a privilege. He does not seek authority for its own sake. He simply struggles to walk away when someone needs help.

## Personality

Zayn's personality changes noticeably depending on who he is with, though the values beneath it remain the same.

As a hunter and team leader, he is disciplined, focused, and highly responsible. He takes planning seriously, prefers clear communication, and feels personally accountable for the safety of both his teammates and the civilians he protects. While he rarely seeks authority, he naturally gravitates toward leadership because he struggles to ignore problems that need solving.

Around friends, however, Zayn is considerably more relaxed. Competitive, playful, and occasionally reckless, he enjoys contests, friendly arguments, and opportunities to set responsibility aside for a while. He values these moments deeply, viewing them as a chance to recover from the pressure he places on himself. Many people who know him only through his work are surprised by how easygoing he can be outside of it.

With family and close friends, a softer side emerges. Zayn enjoys taking care of people in practical ways, whether by cooking, repairing equipment, helping with projects, or quietly checking on someone who seems troubled. He often notices problems before they are mentioned and has a habit of helping without being asked. While not especially expressive with words, he shows affection through actions and reliability.

His kindness is practical rather than dramatic. Instead of grand gestures, he focuses on small acts of care repeated consistently over time. To Zayn, loyalty is not something declared. It is something demonstrated.

Despite his emotional maturity, Zayn often struggles to distinguish responsibility from obligation. He finds it difficult to walk away when someone needs help and frequently assumes burdens that do not belong to him. While this makes him dependable, it can also leave him exhausted, frustrated, and carrying problems that were never his to solve.

## Backstory

Zayn was born in Xobbote, a volcanic nation known for its master smiths, before moving to Zhoter as a child when his father accepted a position with a major forging company. Growing up, he divided his admiration between two people: his father, who used his craft to support others, and his older brother, a hunter who dedicated his life to protecting communities. Though their professions differed, Zayn saw the same lesson in both of them: strength had value only when it was used in service of others.

That belief became the foundation of his identity.

One of the defining moments of his childhood came when he befriended Alex Doyle, who struggled with prosopagnosia. To make himself easier to recognize, Zayn began wearing the same distinctive cape every day despite the ridicule it attracted from other children. What began as a simple gesture of friendship eventually became a symbol of loyalty shared by Alex and later Emília, forming one of the earliest foundations of Team Vulcan.

As he grew older, Zayn's sense of responsibility intensified. His mother's illness forced the family to confront challenges that could not be solved through determination alone, while the eventual departure of both his father and older brother left him wrestling with questions he never fully answered. The people who taught him the importance of responsibility had chosen paths that took them elsewhere. Though he understood their reasons, part of him struggled with the feeling of being left behind.

Rather than abandoning the lessons they taught him, Zayn embraced them even more strongly. Over time he adopted a belief that would shape much of his life: if someone you care about is suffering, you stay.

That conviction eventually led him toward monster hunting. What began as admiration for his brother evolved into a personal commitment to protecting others, not because he believed himself heroic, but because he could not comfortably do otherwise.

His forge hammer symbolizes the discipline and perseverance he inherited from his family. His cape symbolizes something equally important: the belief that loyalty is built through small acts of kindness repeated consistently over time.

## Character Flaws

Zayn believes preparation prevents suffering.

Because of this, he places enormous value on planning, discipline, and clearly defined responsibilities. Before a mission, he wants contingencies. During a mission, he wants communication. After a mission, he wants to understand what could have been done better. To Zayn, preparation is not simply a professional habit. It is an act of care. Every precaution taken is another chance to prevent someone from being hurt.

This mindset makes him an effective leader. He is reliable under pressure, rarely caught unprepared, and constantly thinking several steps ahead. Teammates trust him because they know he takes their safety seriously and will do everything in his power to bring them home.

However, that same mindset can become a weakness.

Zayn struggles with uncertainty and often becomes frustrated when others ignore plans, act impulsively, or introduce variables he cannot account for. He prefers structure over improvisation and can be slow to trust unconventional solutions, especially when the stakes are high. While he recognizes that flexibility is sometimes necessary, accepting it emotionally is far more difficult than accepting it intellectually.

More importantly, Zayn has a habit of assuming responsibility for outcomes that were never fully within his control. When things go wrong, his first instinct is often to search for what he could have done differently. He replays decisions, revisits plans, and looks for mistakes even when none exist. To him, failure rarely feels random. It feels personal.

Part of this stems from his upbringing. Growing up around people who used their skills to help others, Zayn internalized the belief that strength exists to prevent suffering. Over time, that belief evolved into an unspoken assumption: if someone is hurt, then perhaps he should have done more.

This makes it difficult for him to accept situations that cannot be solved through effort, preparation, or determination alone. He understands logically that some outcomes are unavoidable. Emotionally, he often feels responsible anyway.

As a result, Zayn frequently carries burdens that do not belong to him. He takes on extra responsibilities, worries about problems before they occur, and struggles to let others handle situations themselves. While his dedication inspires trust and loyalty, it can also leave him exhausted, frustrated, and convinced that every setback is somehow his fault.

His greatest challenge is learning that responsibility has limits. Not every problem can be prevented. Not every person can be protected. And sometimes caring about others means accepting that their struggles are not his to carry.

## Abilities

Zayn is a highly versatile hunter whose greatest strength lies in the combination of disciplined swordsmanship, blacksmithing expertise, and powerful lava bending. While capable of immense destruction, he is known for his restraint, preferring precise and controlled solutions over overwhelming force.

His primary weapon is a greatsword of his own design and construction. Years spent working in his family's forge gave him not only the ability to craft weapons, but also a deep understanding of how they function. Zayn is an accomplished swordsman, emphasizing efficiency, timing, and defense over flashy techniques. His fighting style focuses on controlling engagements, protecting allies, and creating openings rather than pursuing aggression for its own sake.

As a lava bender, Zayn can manipulate both heat and molten rock. Unlike many wielders of similarly destructive powers, he uses these abilities with exceptional care. In most situations, he relies on controlled applications of heat to strengthen attacks, weaken enemy equipment, or influence the battlefield without causing unnecessary collateral damage. Fully unleashing his lava bending is something he reserves for situations where there is no safer alternative.

Beyond combat, Zayn is a skilled blacksmith and craftsman. He forges and maintains his own equipment, constantly refining his weapons and armor to better suit his needs. This knowledge also gives him a practical understanding of materials, construction, and structural weaknesses, allowing him to assess both equipment and environments with a craftsman's eye.

As Team Vulcan's leader, Zayn has developed strong tactical and organizational abilities. He excels at planning operations, coordinating allies, and adapting strategies under pressure. While not the most academically minded member of the team, he possesses strong practical judgment and a talent for bringing together people with different strengths toward a common goal.

His greatest asset, however, is not any single skill or power. It is his reliability. Whether in combat, leadership, or everyday life, Zayn is someone others can depend on: a person who remains calm under pressure, stands beside those who need him, and consistently places the well-being of others before his own.

## Relationships

### Alex

Alex was Zayn's first friend, predating Team Vulcan, hunting, and leadership entirely. Zayn's earliest memories include helping him navigate prosopagnosia. They think alike: Alex gathers patterns and information, Zayn decides what to do with them, and years of that division built a kind of teamwork that doesn't need much explaining. Alex notices what others miss, whether in Ether theory or in people, and missions have gone smoother more than once because of it, though he rarely credits himself the way he should, and Zayn has learned to listen closely whenever he speaks.

The friendship doesn't need constant contact. Alex can vanish into research for days, Zayn can disappear into guild work, and neither worries. It has lasted too long to need reassurance. As teammates, Alex sees the planner under the easygoing surface and the stubbornness under the patience; Zayn sees strengths in Alex that Alex routinely overlooks in himself. They balance each other: Alex helps him understand the world, Zayn helps him act on it. No single defining moment built this friendship. Just years of quiet consistency, and a constant Zayn never has to question.

### Emília

Emília has been around for almost as long as Alex, and what Zayn values most in her is that she never accepts something just because someone else sounds confident about it. If a plan has a flaw, she says so. If something doesn't track, she asks. As a leader, it would have been easy to surround himself with people who simply agreed, but Emília never let that happen, and her questions regularly surfaced assumptions he didn't know he was making, without ever undermining him.

Her curiosity is relentless. One explanation tends to spawn three more questions, then a project, then a problem nobody saw coming, and rather than finding it exhausting, Zayn finds it expansive. They balance each other: he brings structure when her excitement outruns the plan, she brings flexibility when he gets too attached to the plan itself. They dated once, decided they worked better as friends, and the breakup left no resentment behind, only proof of how much they cared. As teammates, they can argue a plan for an hour, fail to convince each other, and walk away fine, because the disagreement was never a threat to the trust underneath it. She's never let him stop asking questions, and he's better for it.

### Susie

Susie was once unreadable: sharp, guarded, and determined to keep the world at arm's length. By the time the masks came off and Team Lupin joined the guild, Zayn discovered the person beneath the walls, and what struck him wasn't how different she was, but how much she cared. She noticed what others missed, remembered details, and looked after people quietly, never asking for credit. With Sokah and Yel, that care became a home: she never treated them as obligations, just made room for them, meal by meal, until two children who needed a family had one.

She also saw him: not the leader, not the dependable hunter, just Zayn. She clocked his exhaustion before he voiced it and never once treated it as weakness. Their relationship became an exchange: he gave her certainty she never had to earn, and she taught him that being loved and being needed weren't the same thing, that support could be gentle, and letting someone help carry the weight was its own kind of strength. Around her, there was no role to perform. By the time he realized he loved her, it didn't feel surprising. It felt like coming home.

### Sokah

Sokah started as a boy who needed help: new to Zhoter, overwhelmed by the language, trying to learn everything at once. The lessons were meant to be temporary. They weren't. He reminded Zayn of himself at that age: hardworking, curious, carrying more than he should have to. Sokah looked up to him completely, which made Zayn careful. He never wanted to be mistaken for perfect, so he let Sokah see his flaws on purpose. He wanted him to learn that good people get tired, make mistakes, and need help, and none of that makes them less worthy of love.

What started as language lessons became lessons in judgment and belonging, and Zayn slid naturally into the role of older brother. What worried him was how much of Sokah's self-worth hinged on being useful. He volunteered too fast, worked too hard, didn't know how to rest. Zayn recognized the pattern because he lived it too, and tried to teach Sokah what he was still learning himself: that value isn't earned by what you provide. In return, Sokah pulled him toward laughter and small joys he'd otherwise overlook. Somewhere along the way, the boy who needed help with a language became family.

### Yel

Yel said little, and most people read that as distance. Zayn never did. He came to see her as one of the most affectionate people he knew, just fluent in a different language. She spoke through presence: sitting nearby, leaning into people she trusted, content to share a room in total silence. Some of his favorite memories of her involve almost no words at all, such as her in the forge, reading or watching the fire while he worked.

Part of the bond was simple physics: she ran cold, he ran warm, and as a lava bender he was an easy source of heat she happily stole. Physical closeness was how she expressed trust when words felt too complicated. They built a private language of small rituals, such as matching clock numbers, odd bus routes, and bad jokes that took her a beat to land, that belonged to no one else. What worried him was how much of her life had been about survival, how rarely she asked for comfort outright, so he made certain things obvious instead: she was welcome, and affection never needed to be earned. There was no single moment she became his sister. Just a thousand small ones, until protecting her quietly turned into having her as family.

### Kai

On paper, Zayn and Kai agreed on almost nothing. She trusted instinct; he trusted preparation. She moved first and adjusted later; he planned first and acted after. Neither had ever talked the other out of their approach, and neither had stopped trying. What made it work was what sat underneath: they wanted the same things, to help people and to use strength in service of others, and their fights were about method, never values. He genuinely loved disagreeing with her; she pushed him toward action, he pulled her toward caution.

Kai never once treated him as a leader. To her, he was just Zayn, which let him be more reckless and considerably less mature than his reputation suggested. Their conversations regularly collapsed into pointless arguments and ridiculous competitions, two capable leaders sharing what felt like a single brain cell. Underneath the bickering was a trust they rarely spoke of directly: no matter how much they annoyed each other, he never doubted she'd show up when it mattered. Asked if she was his rival, he'd say yes. Asked if she was one of his closest friends, he'd say yes to that too, and never saw the contradiction.

### Jonathan

What Zayn respected most in Jonathan was how genuine he was: trusting easily, forgiving readily, never performing his own warmth. It sometimes frustrated Zayn, who instinctively prepared for the worst where Jonathan instinctively believed the best. That difference made them useful to each other: Zayn pushed Jonathan to weigh risk and consequence, and Jonathan reminded Zayn that caution can't solve everything, that people sometimes need trust more than protection.

Competition was their shared language: sparring, training, turning ordinary moments into contests just to keep pushing each other further. Jonathan never mistook the challenge for criticism; he understood it as respect, which let Zayn be bluntly honest without fear of damaging the friendship. Outside of training, Jonathan had a gift for pulling people together, with gatherings, nights out, and photography trips, and Zayn, who always claimed to be too busy, usually ended up there anyway, alongside Kai, enjoying himself more than he'd admit. He never saw Jonathan as merely a rival. He saw a friend who made leadership feel less like protecting people and more like bringing them together.

### Magnus

When Zayn first joined the guild, Magnus was intimidating in every way that mattered: a veteran soldier, a strict leader, someone who seemed impossible to impress. It took years to understand that the harshness came from one simple belief: mistakes cost lives, and every hunter deserved the best possible chance of coming home. Magnus taught him what leadership actually meant. Not authority, not recognition, but responsibility quietly carried because someone had to carry it, the same way Zayn would later carry Team Vulcan. For a long time Zayn assumed Magnus was simply harder on him than on everyone else. Eventually he understood why: the criticism was never disappointment. It was expectation. Magnus pushed because he believed Zayn could meet it.

What Zayn came to admire wasn't just the strength but what it was protecting. Beneath the stern commander was a man who fought for his hunters, checked on them when they struggled, and treated the guild as a family he was responsible for keeping safe. Over the years Magnus became more than a leader: a mentor, a measure Zayn held himself against, someone whose approval mattered more than almost anyone's. Neither of them would say it outright, but at times the relationship sat very close to family.

### Theros

Theros was one of Zayn's oldest friends, and the friendship never needed much upkeep. Different paths, years apart, and they'd still slip back into conversation as if no time had passed. What he valued most was how little the friendship asked of him. Most of his relationships came with responsibilities attached; at Theros's bar he wasn't Team Vulcan's leader or a hunter, just Zayn. It was built on quiet conversation and mutual trust. Theros listened without trying to fix anything, which gave Zayn room to be honest about doubts he rarely voiced elsewhere, and the trust ran both ways.

Zayn also respected the life Theros had quietly built for himself. While most of their friends chased ambitious careers, Theros found purpose behind a bar, bringing it the same care he'd once given alchemy, and turned the place into somewhere people genuinely wanted to be, eventually one of Zayn's favorite spots in the city. Whether he needed advice, company, or just somewhere to think, he knew it was there. More than that, he knew Theros would be there too, asking nothing of him but his presence.
