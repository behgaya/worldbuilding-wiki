The North of [[zhoter]] is a cold, misty land of plains and plateaus, where industry and nature still fight over territory. Its height and its closeness to the mountains give it long winters, frequent snow and summers that rarely pass 20°C. It holds the capital, [[riquehen]], along with [[kubetz]] and [[trovic]], and it is the beating heart of the country: dense, vertical and covered in a skin of burnt copper.

## Landscape

The northern plains were once fertile and damp. Today the land lies hidden under layers of metal and steam, with rails, towers and conduits spreading across it like glowing veins. Only at the edges of the horizon can the original soil still be seen, in distant fields and hills covered in mist.

## Cities

[[riquehen]] and [[kubetz]] rise on mist-covered plateaus, fed by roaring furnaces and chimneys that breathe steam day and night. They are built in vertical layers: darkened copper covers the façades, and walkways of worn bronze link the highest floors. From afar the cityscape seems endless, a sea of buildings, towers and smoking gears. By day the sky looks pale and distant. At night it becomes an orange mirror, reflecting the amber lights that flicker over the steam like stars forged in hot metal.

Unstable power grids keep the cities running, and locomotives roar beneath suspended bridges. Progress here smells of hot oil and metallic rain, of wet earth and boiling metal.

[[trovic]] is different. Smaller and built around its community, it has none of the towering layers of the big cities. Its steam and machinery are modest, and the warm glow of its workshops and lamps stands out against the cold, pale landscape around it.

## Life

For all its dense buildings and smoky air, the North is alive. Its wide streets cut through the mist like rivers of light, open to a constant flow of light locomotives, steam carriages and crowds that never stop moving. Between the bronze and the steel there are bustling markets, invention fairs and festivals of light that turn the steam into a spectacle. Even under the weight of the machines, northerners still find time to laugh, create and celebrate.

## The Forest of Yaga

North of the cities lies the Forest of Yaga, the last living frontier, an ancient wood that the march of progress has never touched. Its tall conifers shine with a faint metallic tint in the damp, marked by the ores and underground vapors that soak the soil. Legends speak of spirits trapped in the roots. To northerners, Yaga is a sacred boundary, or perhaps a cursed one: a reminder that not everything can be shaped by steam. Cutting its trees is taboo, and so is entering it. Going in is not impossible, and a few have done it, but the forest is seen as cursed and almost no one tries. Shrines and warnings mark its edges, and stories circulate quietly of people who went too far in and came back changed.

## The Sek mountains

Beyond the forest, the Sek mountains rise like bluish walls, covered in snow and in crystals that catch the light like distant mirrors. They mark the border with Sek'luz and Mughierel, lands that are barely known in Zhoter. No trade passes through the mountains, and few, if any, have ever crossed them successfully. For Zhoter, they are the edge of knowledge itself. It is said that up there the air grows so thin that not even steam can rise.

Nature still holds on among the gears, but each day it seems weaker against the smoke that covers the horizon. The North is a land of bronze and mist, where human ingenuity shines in shades of old gold before fading into soot.
