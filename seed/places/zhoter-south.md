The South of [[zhoter]] is a mosaic of fertile plains, forests, rivers and warm lagoons that run down to the sea. It lies at sea level, far from the northern mountains, so its climate is the mildest in the country: warmer than the rest of Zhoter but still temperate, humid and rainy, with mist lingering between the trees. Its living rains (electric storms charged with Ether) light up the sky and feed a land of abundance and creativity. It is home to [[telis]], [[solnos]] and [[gatusou]], and it is the country's granary, providing the grains, fruit, herbs and etheric oils that sustain both people and machines. Here, Ether is not extracted or forced: it falls from the sky, seeps into the soil and grows with the crops.

## Land and reuse

The rich soil is constantly renewed by recycled waste from the North, brought by barge and airship. What arrives as industrial scrap becomes farm tools, irrigation turbines and improvised harvesters. In the same way, old etheric treatises, fragments of doctrine and broken devices are reused as study material, because southerners believe all knowledge can sprout again. The South lives on transformation: ingenuity takes the place of luxury, and necessity inspires art and learning.

## Ether and bending

Southerners see Ether as a living, cyclical force, closely tied to the land, the rain and the body. To them, bending Ether is not imposing one's will but listening, harmonizing and giving back. Bending is taught as an extension of daily life: channeling to irrigate, strengthening the soil, protecting villages from excessive storms, or healing the exhaustion of heavy labor.

Houses of Etheric Study are scattered across the South: simple structures of wood, clay and reused metal, open to the community. They have no walls and no rigid uniforms. Masters and students live together, learning through observation, practice and shared life. Young people from all over Zhoter come south to study, drawn by the emotional stability and the etherically fertile environment, where mistakes are not punished with violence but treated as part of the cycle of learning.

## Students of Ether

Southern students learn early to recognize the signs of imbalance: a field that grows too fast, a rain that will not stop, a body that channels beyond its limit. Emotional control matters as much as technique, and many exercises involve music, dance, cultivation and simple rituals performed before the storms. During the living rains, whole groups gather in the fields to absorb and redistribute Ether, turning the natural phenomenon into a collective lesson.

For all its peaceful outlook, the South is not naive. It knows the North watches its students with interest and the West sees practical uses for their abilities. That is why its masters teach clear limits: Ether must serve life, never be reduced to a weapon or pure fuel. Those who want to follow other paths leave, but never without understanding the weight of what they carry.

## Culture and faith

Life in the South is marked by harvests, fairs and celebrations, where country music mixes with the hiss of gears and the soft glow of channeled Ether. Small altars appear in the villages, dedicated not to gods but to bending in balance, represented by simple symbols carved in wood or shaped in clay. No one asks them for power; people give thanks for the harmony that has been kept.

## Abundance and inequality

The South's abundance does not reach everyone who works for it. Its harvests feed the whole country, but the farmland is concentrated around [[telis]], the most farm-focused state and the home of the South's wealthy. There, landowners run the trade and make the decisions about harvests and tariffs, while workers live in satellite villages with little infrastructure. The rest of the South is more naturalistic: forest, rivers and mist, where villages and wild land blur into each other. The food routes cross [[gatusou]] and almost nothing comes back. The Ether culture and the Houses of Study belong to the villages and their people, as do the festivals of [[solnos]]: what the South keeps for itself is not its wealth, but its knowledge.

Far from the political disputes of the North, the South is the organic pulse of Zhoter, the place where Ether is still treated with reverence and care. Between steam and clay, southerners grow not only what sustains the country, but what may save it: knowledge, balance, and the memory that all power needs roots.
