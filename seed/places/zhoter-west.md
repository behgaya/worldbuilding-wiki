The West of [[zhoter]] is a vast sea of dark grass and rolling hills under a sky almost always veiled in haze. It is the heart of Zhoter's ranching and the country's first shield, home to the military city of [[avernist]] and the border city of [[driogoir]], with the nation of Arzenerra just beyond the sandstone canyons.

## Landscape

The West is green pampa, not dry steppe. Rain falls throughout the year, summers are hot and humid, and winters are cold, with frost on the grass at dawn and fog settling in the low valleys.

The haze overhead, though, is not natural. It is the steam and soot of the Flux Posts, which build an oppressive sky of ash and mauve and force the sun to fight for every ray of golden light. On clear days, signal flags and observation balloons float between the towers, watching for any movement from the border.

The flatness of the pampa, a symbol of freedom, is broken by the grim verticality of technology. At strategic points stand the Central Vacuum Towers, colossal rusted structures covered in decaying latticework, like ruins of a forgotten industrial age. Besides pulsing with etheric energy and steam, many of them serve as watch and defense posts, armed with pressure cannons, long-range searchlights and landing platforms for mobile patrols.

The land opens into golden fields and wide valleys, where the cold north wind blows through the tall grass and carries the metallic ring of horseshoes. Rails wind across the pampa, past automated farms and pastures divided by electric fences that, in an alert, can be powered up and closed into containment lines. Nature keeps trying to take back what was taken from it: grass grows over the metal, even in old trenches and abandoned artillery platforms from past conflicts.

## The Mechanical Gauchos

Along these routes travel caravans of hybrid cattle, sturdy beasts of flesh and copper whose reddish plates glow against the grey. They are driven by the Mechanical Gauchos, herders and warriors who pair the traditional lasso and bolas with pressure exoskeletons, steam rifles and mounts that are half organic, half forged. In peacetime they look like simple drovers guarding their herds. In times of tension they quickly become mounted militias, able to regroup and strike before the enemy notices them.

## Economy

The West lives on automated ranching, reinforced leather, synthetic milk and biothermal energy drawn from elemental beasts. The farm-towns that dot the plains are linked by rails and steam conduits fed by the Flux Posts, and many were designed to double as military supply points, storing provisions, parts and fuel for long campaigns on the frontier.

## Culture

What keeps the West alive is not only what it produces, but its free spirit. Despite constant patrols and armed readiness, westerners keep a spiritual bond with the land and the wind. They believe the western wind speaks with the dead, and that its metallic howl through scrap turbines and tower antennas carries the voices of the warriors and herders who defended the pampa before them.

Sunset festivals are the heart of western culture. In the brief glow that breaks through the veil of steam, people celebrate with electric violins, pressure drums and spinning dances among sparks and smoke. Between songs, stories of recent battles mix with old legends, and training happens almost unnoticed: in the spin of a bola, a precise shot, complete control of a mount. In those moments the pampa is free again, even knowing it could be called to war at any moment.

## The border

With Arzenerra just past the sandstone canyons, the West is a vital point of cultural and technological exchange, and also Zhoter's first shield. Its trade in reinforced leather, light alloys and hybrid mounts sustains the country, while its people carry something heavier: the certainty that if conflict comes, the West will feel it first.
